The Big Picture

  • Google turned search into one of the most profitable businesses in history. Its parent company, Alphabet, now competes on many fronts.
  • Search works by crawling the web, indexing pages, and ranking them. PageRank treats links as votes. Firms compete for rank with SEO.
  • Most of Google's money comes from search ads sold in an auction where quality matters as much as the bid.
  • Google's ad network places ads on millions of other sites using contextual advertising.
  • Generative AI is both Google's biggest opportunity and a threat to its core business, which fits the classic disruption pattern.
Your goals

What You’ll Learn

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Explain how Google's search ad auction works, including pay-per-click, quality score, and CTR.

  • Explain why generative AI threatens Google's search business and how Google is responding.
  • Evaluate Alphabet's other businesses (Android, YouTube, Cloud, Waymo) and the challenges they face.
Section 16.1

Why Study Google?

Founded in 1998 by Stanford grad students Larry Page and Sergey Brin, Google became the world's dominant search engine and one of the most valuable firms by market cap. In 2015 it reorganized under a parent company, Alphabet, so it could manage “moonshots” like self-driving cars separately from search.

Why it matters

Google shows nearly every concept in this course in action: network effects, scale, data as an asset, platforms, the cloud, AI, privacy, regulation, and now the threat of disruption to its own core business.

Section 16.2

Understanding Search

  1. CrawlSoftware robots called spiders or web crawlers follow links from page to page, discovering content.
  2. IndexGoogle stores and organizes what it finds, like a giant book index, in a cache across its data centers.
  3. RankWhen you search (a query), algorithms rank the matching pages. These unpaid results are called organic (or natural) search.
Algorithm

PageRank

Larry Page's insight: a link to a page is like a vote. Links from important pages count more. Today Google uses hundreds of signals.

Marketing

SEO

Search engine optimization means improving a page so it ranks higher in organic results, with useful content, fast pages, and good links.

Watch out

Link fraud

Fake sites built just to link to each other (“link farms”) to game rankings. Google can punish cheaters by dropping them from results entirely.

Behind the scenes · Google's infrastructure

Google runs huge, custom-built data centers around the world, designed for fault tolerance: when servers fail, others take over instantly. Its scale and engineering are a competitive advantage that's very hard to copy. Some content, in the deep web, can't be indexed at all.

Section 16.3

Search Advertising: How Google Makes Money

The ads above and beside search results are keyword ads, matched to what you searched. Advertisers usually pay per click (PPC), and designing these campaigns is called search engine marketing (SEM). Ads people are searching for right now are extremely valuable. The searcher is telling Google exactly what they want.

The ad auction: it's not just the highest bid

Google ranks ads using the bid and a quality score. The quality score is based on expected click-through rate (CTR), the ad's relevance, and the landing page experience. Here's a simplified example:

Simplified ad rank = max bid × quality score
AdvertiserMax bid per clickQuality score (1–10)Ad rankPosition
Shoe Shop A$4.003123rd
Shoe Shop B$2.009181st
Shoe Shop C$3.005152nd
Why this design is smart

Shop B wins the top spot while bidding the least, because people actually click its relevant ad. Google earns money only on clicks, so relevant ads mean more revenue for Google and a better experience for searchers.

Challenge · Mobile apps

On phones, people often skip the search box and go straight to apps like Amazon, TikTok, or Instagram. Content inside apps is harder for Google to crawl, although deep linking lets search results open a specific spot inside an app.

Section 16.4

The Google Ad Network: Beyond Search

Google's ad network places ads on millions of partner websites, apps, and YouTube videos, and shares the revenue with publishers. It uses contextual advertising, matching ads to the content of the page, like camping gear ads on a hiking blog.

Advertisers win

Huge reach

One campaign can reach people almost anywhere online, and AI tools now help create and target the ads.

Publishers win

Easy money

Even tiny niche sites can earn ad revenue without a sales team.

Google wins

Network effects

More advertisers attract more publishers, and more publishers attract more advertisers. That's a two-sided network.

Watch out · The content adjacency problem

Automated placement can put a brand's ad next to offensive or tragic content, such as an airline ad beside a plane-crash story. Advertisers use negative keywords and site exclusions to avoid this.

Section 16.5

Generative AI and the Future of Search

When ChatGPT arrived in late 2022, people suddenly had a new way to get answers, often without clicking a single link or ad. That's a threat to Google's main business.

Irony

Google invented the tech

Google researchers created the 2017 “Transformer” design behind modern LLMs, but OpenAI turned it into a hit product first.

Disruption?

The classic pattern

Chatbots began as “worse” at facts but better at conversation, and they're improving fast. Google had a big reason to move cautiously: it didn't want to undercut its search ad profits.

Response

AI everywhere

Google launched its Gemini models and AI-generated answers at the top of search, and built AI into Gmail, Docs, Android, and Google Cloud.

Why it matters to you · Be a critical AI user

AI answers can be wrong, outdated, or biased, and they may not show you where the information came from. Check the sources, compare answers, and use your own judgment, especially for school, health, money, and legal questions.

Section 16.6

Alphabet’s Multi-Front Battle

As search matures, Alphabet looks for growth elsewhere. Technology makes industries converge, so Google now competes with Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and others all at once.

Alphabet's major bets
BusinessWhat it isKey challenge
AndroidThe world's most-used mobile OS, free to phone makersApple's profitable iPhone ecosystem and antitrust scrutiny
YouTubeA giant video platform with ads and subscriptionsTikTok, content moderation, and creator competition
Google PayMobile payments using NFC tap-to-payApple Pay's lead and the banks
Google CloudCloud and AI services for businessesTrails AWS and Microsoft Azure
WaymoSelf-driving taxis now operating in several U.S. citiesSafety, regulation, public trust, and cost
Watch out · Regulators and global challenges

In 2024, a U.S. federal judge ruled that Google illegally maintained a monopoly in search, partly through deals that made it the default search engine on phones and browsers. Courts have also challenged its ad tech business. Around the world, Google faces privacy rules, censorship demands, and government pressure. Being a giant brings giant scrutiny.

Vocabulary

Key Terms

Want every term from every module in one place? Try the searchable glossary.

Query
A search request.
Organic (natural) search
Unpaid search results ranked by relevance.
PageRank
Google's original algorithm that ranks pages partly by the number and importance of links pointing to them.
SEO
Search engine optimization: improving a page so it ranks higher in organic results.
Link fraud
Creating fake sites that link to each other to manipulate search rankings. Also called link farming.
Spider (web crawler)
Software that follows links across the web to discover and index pages.
Search engine marketing (SEM)
Designing, running, and optimizing search ad campaigns.
Keyword advertising
Ads targeted to the words a user searches for.
Pay-per-click (PPC)
A model where advertisers pay only when someone clicks their ad.
Quality score
Google's rating of an ad's expected click-through rate, relevance, and landing page experience.
Click-through rate (CTR)
The percentage of people who click an ad after seeing it: clicks ÷ impressions.
Landing page
The web page a user reaches after clicking an ad.
Deep linking
A link that opens a specific page or spot inside a site or app instead of its home screen.
Contextual advertising
Ads matched to the content of the page where they appear.
Content adjacency problem
When an ad appears next to content the advertiser wants to avoid.
Negative keywords
Words that stop an ad from showing when they appear.
Convergence
When separate markets start offering the same features and begin competing.
NFC
Near field communication: short-range wireless used for tap-to-pay.
