The Big Picture

  • Artificial intelligence is software that can do tasks that normally take human intelligence. Generative AI creates new text, images, audio, and video from a prompt.
  • Large language models learn patterns from a huge training corpus. They predict likely next words, which makes them powerful and also able to hallucinate.
  • GenAI is changing nearly every job, from coding and design to accounting and law. Agentic AI can now plan and take actions on its own.
  • Real risks today include bias, deepfakes, security attacks, copyright questions, job shocks, and energy use.
  • Responsible AI takes testing, oversight, red teams, and clear policies, plus managers who ask hard questions.

Sections 12.1–12.2

Generative AI: This Is Something Different

AI has been around since the 1950s, with bursts of hype followed by AI winters, periods when interest and funding dried up. Then, in late 2022, OpenAI's ChatGPT reached millions of users within days. For the first time, anyone could type a prompt and get a fluent essay, code, or image.

Untangling the names
TermWhat it is
Generative AIThe category: AI that creates new media (text, images, audio, video)
GPTOpenAI's family of models (Generative Pre-trained Transformer)
ChatGPTThe product: a chat app built on GPT models
OthersGoogle Gemini, Anthropic Claude, Meta Llama, Microsoft Copilot (which uses OpenAI models), DeepSeek, and more
Real-world example · Microsoft's big bet

Microsoft invested billions in OpenAI and built its models into Bing, Windows, and Office as “Copilot,” bringing GenAI to hundreds of millions of workers.

Section 12.3

How Does This Stuff Really Work?

How a large language model is built

  1. Gather a corpusCollect a massive amount of training text from books, websites, code, and more.
  2. Train a foundation modelA neural network learns patterns in how words relate, stored as billions of parameters. Google's 2017 “Transformer” design made this possible.
  3. Refine itHumans rate answers to tune the model's behavior (RLHF), or the model is trained to follow written principles (constitutional AI).
  4. Prompt itUsers type requests. The model predicts the most likely next words, one piece at a time.
Learning type

Supervised

Trained on labeled examples, like “this email is spam” and “this one isn't.”

Learning type

Self-supervised

Finds patterns in unlabeled data on its own. LLMs learn this way by predicting missing or next words.

Older AI

Expert systems

Hand-written rules that mimic an expert's decisions, like early medical diagnosis tools.

Future?

AGI

Artificial general intelligence: AI that could learn any task like a human. It doesn't exist yet, and experts disagree on when or whether it will.

Watch out · Hallucinations

LLMs predict what sounds right, not what is right. They can confidently invent facts, sources, or legal cases. Those are hallucinations. Always verify important outputs.

Real-world example · DeepSeek

In early 2025, Chinese startup DeepSeek released a model that rivaled top U.S. models and was reportedly trained at a fraction of the cost. It shook assumptions that only firms with the biggest budgets could compete.

Section 12.4

GenAI: Changing Products and Work Itself

GenAI across the workplace
AreaWhat's changing
Office workAI drafts emails, summarizes meetings, builds slides, and analyzes spreadsheets.
ProgrammingTools like GitHub Copilot act like a pair programmer. “Vibe coding” means building apps by prompting AI, often without a formal plan.
DesignAdobe Firefly and similar tools generate and edit images from text.
Accounting & lawAI reviews documents, extracts data (with tools like OCR), and drafts first versions.
Customer serviceChatbots answer questions, but poorly designed bots can frustrate customers and damage a brand.
Key idea · Agentic AI

Agentic AI doesn't just answer. It can plan steps, use tools, and take actions toward a goal with little human oversight, like booking travel or fixing code. That's powerful, and mistakes can now have real consequences.

Think like a manager · Build or license?

Licensing a model from OpenAI, Google, or Anthropic is fast and powerful. Building or customizing your own, often from open models like Llama, gives you more control over data and costs, but it takes talent and computing power.

Sections 12.5–12.6

Understanding AI Risks (Today, Not Sci-Fi)

Security

Prompt injection

Tricking an AI with crafted prompts so it ignores its rules or leaks data.

Security

Data poisoning

Feeding bad training data on purpose so the AI produces wrong results.

Fairness

Bias

AI learns from human-created data, so it can repeat stereotypes, like a translator that assumes doctors are “he.”

Deception

Deepfakes

Realistic fake audio and video. Scammers clone voices to impersonate family members.

Legal

Copyright

Authors and artists are suing over whether training on their work without permission is legal. It's still unsettled.

Society

Jobs, power & energy

Job disruption, a few giant firms gaining power, and data centers that use enormous amounts of electricity and water.

Watch out · The black box

Neural networks make decisions through billions of connections, and even their creators can't fully explain a given answer. That's a problem when AI is used for loans, hiring, or medical decisions.

Real-world example · Hollywood strikes

In 2023, Hollywood writers and actors went on strike partly over AI. They won limits on how studios can use AI to write scripts or copy actors' likenesses. Industries with strong unions and hard-to-replace talent have more power to push back.

Even when AI works, adopting it is hard. Firms need clean data, skilled people, and change management to help employees adapt to new ways of working.

Section 12.7

Building More Responsible AI

  1. Set standardsCreate clear AI policies, and use governance frameworks like COBIT and ITIL.
  2. Check the dataAudit training data for bias, gaps, and privacy problems.
  3. Red team itHave an independent team try to break or misuse the system before launch.
  4. Keep humans in the loopRequire human review for high-stakes decisions.
  5. Monitor and improveKeep testing after launch. Let users report problems.
Real-world example · Big data catches a killer

Investigators identified the “Golden State Killer” decades later by matching crime-scene DNA with relatives' profiles on a public genealogy site. It shows the promise of big data, and the privacy questions it raises when data is used in ways people never expected.

Section 12.8 · Case

Case: AI as Your DJ at Spotify

Spotify bought the music-data firm The Echo Nest in 2014 to strengthen its recommendation skills. Its AI combines several kinds of data:

Data 1

Listening behavior

What you play, skip, save, and add to playlists, compared with millions of similar listeners.

Data 2

The audio itself

Tempo, energy, “danceability,” and other features analyzed from each track.

Data 3

What people write

Blogs, reviews, and playlist titles that describe songs and artists.

The result is personalized features like Discover Weekly and an AI DJ that talks to you between songs. Personalization is a big reason listeners stay. Every play makes the recommendations better, which makes the service harder to leave.

Vocabulary

Key Terms

Want every term from every module in one place? Try the searchable glossary.

Artificial intelligence (AI)
Software that can perform or improve on tasks that normally require human intelligence.
AI winter
A period when AI interest and funding dropped after the technology failed to live up to the hype.
Generative AI
AI that creates new content like text, images, audio, or video in response to a prompt.
Prompt
The request a user types or speaks to a generative AI system.
Prompt engineering
Designing prompts that get the best results from generative AI.
Hallucination
An incorrect or made-up answer that an AI presents as fact.
Corpus
The body of data used to train an AI model.
Parameters
The values a model learns during training that shape how it generates output.
Neural network
A statistical technique, loosely inspired by the brain, that finds patterns through layers of connections.
Machine learning (ML)
AI that improves its predictions by learning from data instead of being explicitly programmed.
Deep learning
Machine learning that uses many-layered neural networks.
Large language model (LLM)
AI trained on huge amounts of text to understand and generate language.
Foundation model
A general-purpose model that can be adapted for many specific tasks.
AGI
Artificial general intelligence: hypothetical AI that could learn any task the way a human can.
Turing test
The idea of testing whether a computer's behavior is indistinguishable from a human's.
Supervised learning
Machine learning trained on labeled examples of the right answers.
Self-supervised learning
Machine learning that finds patterns in unlabeled data on its own.
RLHF
Reinforcement learning from human feedback: tuning a model using human ratings of its answers.
Constitutional AI
Training an AI to follow a written set of principles for safer, more aligned behavior.
Agentic AI
AI that can independently plan, decide, and take actions toward a goal with little human oversight.
Vibe coding
Building software by repeatedly prompting AI, often without a formal plan or spec.
Prompt injection
An attack that uses crafted prompts to make an AI ignore its rules or leak data.
Data poisoning
Deliberately feeding bad data to an AI so it produces wrong results.
Deepfake
Realistic AI-generated audio, images, or video that imitate real people or events.
Change management
Techniques that help people and organizations adapt to new systems and ways of working.
