The Big Picture

  • Facebook grew from a college dorm project into Meta, a family of apps used by billions: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, and Threads.
  • Its core advantages are network effects and switching costs, built on the social graph: the map of who's connected to whom.
  • To win on mobile, Meta bought rivals (Instagram, WhatsApp) and copied features (Stories, Reels).
  • Meta is betting billions on the metaverse, smart glasses, and AI. The losses are large and the payoff isn't certain.
  • Scandals like Cambridge Analytica and the spread of fake news show how platforms, data, and privacy can go badly wrong.

Section 8.1

From Dorm Room to Meta

Mark Zuckerberg launched Facebook at Harvard in 2004. It spread to other colleges, then to everyone. Facebook grew so fast that investors competed to fund it, which let Zuckerberg keep voting control of the company. Its 2012 IPO (first public stock sale) was rocky, but the company became one of the world's most valuable. In 2021, it renamed itself Meta to signal its metaverse ambitions.

App

Facebook

The original social network, still with billions of users.

App

Instagram

Photo and video sharing, bought in 2012 for about $1 billion.

App

WhatsApp

Messaging that's hugely popular worldwide, bought in 2014 for about $19 billion.

App

Messenger & Threads

Chat, plus Threads, a text-based rival to X (Twitter) launched in 2023.

Hardware

Reality Labs

Quest VR headsets and Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses.

Section 8.2

Competitive Advantage: The Social Graph

The social graph is the map of people and organizations and how they're connected. Facebook's is huge and accurate because people use real names and connect with people they actually know.

Resource 1

Network effects

Your friends and family are all there, so that's where you go too.

Resource 2

Switching costs

Years of photos, posts, groups, and connections are painful to rebuild somewhere else.

Features like the News Feed spread content virally and keep people coming back. Facebook also used its massive user base to envelop other services by adding features like photos, events, and video that once required separate apps. Much of its content sits in the deep web, out of reach of search engines like Google.

Behind the scenes · Running at Meta's scale

Serving billions of people takes giant data centers, often in cold places like northern Sweden where outside air helps cool the servers. Meta also uses content delivery networks to speed up loading and builds much of its infrastructure with open source software.

Section 8.3

Meta Goes Mobile and Grows by Acquisition

On the desktop, Facebook controlled the experience. On phones, Apple and Google control the operating systems and app stores, and new apps can grow overnight. Meta responded with big bets:

  1. Buy rising rivalsInstagram and WhatsApp brought in younger users and global messaging before they could become threats.
  2. Copy what worksWhen Snapchat refused to sell, Instagram copied its Stories feature and soon passed Snapchat in Stories users.
  3. Fight TikTok with AIInstagram Reels uses AI recommendations like TikTok does. It's a success, but it hasn't knocked TikTok out.
Watch out · Regulators noticed

U.S. regulators later argued in court that buying Instagram and WhatsApp illegally eliminated competitors. Growth by acquisition can attract antitrust scrutiny.

Section 8.4

The Metaverse and Meta on Your Face

The metaverse describes immersive, shared digital worlds. Meta bought VR headset maker Oculus in 2014 and renamed the whole company after this vision. Its Reality Labs division has lost tens of billions of dollars so far.

Challenge

Comfort & cost

Headsets are still bulky and pricey, and many people don't wear them for long.

Challenge

Killer apps

Gaming and fitness work well. Mainstream reasons to “live” in VR are still unclear.

Bright spot

Ray-Ban Meta glasses

Normal-looking glasses with a camera, speakers, and AI assistant have sold far better than past smart glasses.

Why Meta cares · Owning the platform

Meta depends on Apple's and Google's phone platforms, which can limit what it does (see below). Owning the next computing platform, whether that's headsets or glasses, would put Meta in control.

Section 8.5

AI: Essential to Meta’s Success

AI decides what appears in your feeds, which ads you see, and what content gets removed. Meta has also built:

Language

Llama models

Large language models that Meta releases openly, so other firms can run and customize them on their own computers instead of paying for a closed model.

Images

Emu

Image generation that's built into Meta's apps.

Coding

CodeCompose

An AI coding assistant trained on Meta's own code and used inside its developers' tools (IDEs).

Hardware

Custom chips

Meta designs its own AI chips and spends heavily on data centers to keep up.

Watch out · Red teams

Before releasing AI, firms use red teams, groups that act like attackers and try to make the system misbehave. Openly released models are powerful, but anyone can misuse them, which makes this testing even more important.

Sections 8.6–8.7

Platform Lessons, Privacy Fumbles & Fake News

Facebook became a platform when it opened APIs to outside developers in 2007. Games like FarmVille boomed. But giving partners access to user data also created big risks.

Major missteps and their lessons
EventWhat happenedLesson
Beacon (2007)Facebook shared users' purchases on partner sites with their friends, by default. Users had to opt out.Make sensitive sharing opt-in. Test with real users.
Cambridge Analytica (2018)A quiz app collected data on up to 87 million users, including friends who never used the app, and it ended up with a political consulting firm.A platform is responsible for what partners do with users' data.
Fake newsFalse stories spread widely, some written just to earn ad money and some to influence elections.Algorithms that reward engagement can reward lies. Fixing it takes people and technology, and it's never perfect.
Global comparison · WeChat, the “super app”

In China, WeChat combines messaging, payments, shopping, and government services in one app. That's a walled garden, a closed set of services controlled by one firm. Meta wants Messenger and WhatsApp to grow in this direction.

Watch out · Living on someone else's platform

When Apple let users block app tracking on iPhones, Meta's ad targeting got less precise. Apple also blocked Meta's plans for its own app platform inside iOS. If you build on another firm's platform, it controls your future.

Vocabulary

Key Terms

Want every term from every module in one place? Try the searchable glossary.

IPO
Initial public offering: the first time a company sells shares on a public stock exchange (“going public”).
Short selling
Betting a stock will fall by selling borrowed shares and buying them back later at a lower price.
Social graph
The map of people and organizations and how they're connected to each other.
Envelopment
Adding a rival's product as a feature of your own so users don't need the rival.
Deep web
Online content that search engines like Google can't index.
Content delivery network (CDN)
Servers spread across the Internet that store copies of content close to users so pages and videos load faster.
Open source software
Software that's free and whose code anyone can view and modify.
Metaverse
Immersive, computer-generated worlds where people interact with each other.
Walled garden
A closed network or set of services controlled by one dominant firm.
Red team
A group that plays the attacker to find weaknesses in a system before real bad actors do.
IDE
Integrated development environment: software developers use to write, test, and debug code.
Opt-in
Users must actively agree before a feature or data sharing is turned on.
Opt-out
A feature or data sharing is on by default, and users must act to turn it off.
Fake news
False information presented as real news, often spread for profit or influence.
