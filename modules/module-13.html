The Big Picture

  • Online advertising is booming because it's targetable, measurable, and cheap to test, and attention has shifted to screens.
  • Ads are sold by impressions (CPM), clicks (CPC), or actions (CPA). Firms use zero-, first-, second-, and third-party data to target them.
  • Cookies, tracking pixels, and fingerprinting follow you around the web. Ad networks use that tracking to serve ads across many sites.
  • Apple's App Tracking Transparency made app tracking opt-in, which cost Meta and others billions in ad revenue.
  • Privacy laws like GDPR keep changing. Smart firms build strong data governance instead of waiting to be fined.

Section 13.1

Understanding Online Advertising

People spend more and more time on phones, streaming, and social media, and advertising follows them. Online ads can be targeted to specific people, measured precisely, and changed instantly. Generative AI can even create custom ad creative (the design and content of an ad) for different audiences.

Format

Display ads

Image-based banner ads.

Format

Rich media

Ads with animation, audio, or video.

Format

Interstitials

Ads that appear before you reach the page or app screen you wanted.

Format

Text & search ads

Short text ads, often tied to what you search for.

How online ads are priced
ModelAdvertiser pays when…Best for
CPM (cost per mille)The ad is shown 1,000 times (1,000 impressions)Brand awareness
CPC (cost per click)Someone clicks the adDriving traffic to a site
CPA (cost per action)Someone takes an action, like buying or signing upSales. Affiliate programs use this model.

The four kinds of customer data

  1. Zero-party dataInfo you deliberately give a firm, like your preferences when you sign up.
  2. First-party dataWhat a firm observes from your interactions with it, like your browsing and purchases on its site.
  3. Second-party dataAnother firm's first-party data, shared with a partner.
  4. Third-party dataData gathered by a company with no direct relationship to you, often bought from aggregators.
Section 13.2

Web Tracking: How Ads Follow You

A cookie is a small piece of identifying text that a website stores in your browser. Only the server that set a cookie can read it back.

Cookie

First-party cookie

Set by the site you're visiting. It remembers your login, cart, and preferences.

Cookie

Third-party cookie

Set by a different company, like an ad network, embedded on many sites, so it can follow you across all of them.

Tracker

Tracking pixel

An invisible one-pixel image that reports when you view a page or open an email.

Tracker

Fingerprinting

Identifying your device from details like browser version, fonts, and screen size, with no cookie needed.

Ad tech in flux · Is the third-party cookie dead?

Safari and Firefox block third-party cookies by default. Google spent years planning to phase them out of Chrome, then reversed course and kept them. Advertisers are shifting toward first-party data, contextual ads (matching ads to page content), and other tracking methods, each with its own privacy concerns.

Try it yourself

Open your browser's Developer Tools (F12), then go to Application (or Storage) and Cookies to see the cookies a site stores. Or look at your browser's privacy settings to see which trackers it blocks.

Section 13.3

App Tracking: Apple and Facebook Go to War

iPhone apps used a device ID called the IDFA to track users across apps. In 2021 (iOS 14.5), Apple launched App Tracking Transparency (ATT): every app must ask permission before tracking you.

Opt-in vs. opt-out
Opt-out (before ATT)Opt-in (after ATT)
DefaultTracking is ON unless you turn it offTracking is OFF unless you say yes
ResultMost users are trackedMost users decline tracking
Two sides of the fight

Apple says: privacy is a human right, and users deserve a choice. Meta says: ATT hurts small businesses that rely on targeted ads, and Apple's own ad business benefits. Meta said the change would cost it about $10 billion in a year. Regulators are examining both firms' claims.

Section 13.4

Device Profiling: Where Are You, and What Are You Watching?

Geotargeting shows ads or content based on your location. Geofencing triggers an action when a device enters a defined area, like a coupon when you walk near a store.

Ways to locate a device
MethodHow it worksAccuracy
IP addressLooks up the region assigned to your Internet address. VPNs and proxy servers can hide it.Rough, usually city level
Wi-FiMatches nearby Wi-Fi networks against a database of known locationsGood, especially indoors
Cell towersMeasures signals from several towers (triangulation)Moderate
GPSSatellite signals. Needs a clear view of the sky (line-of-sight).Very precise outdoors
Watch out · Your TV is watching you

Many smart TVs use ACR (automated content recognition) to identify everything on screen, even from cable or a game console, and sell that viewing data to advertisers. DAI (dynamic ad insertion) then swaps in ads targeted to your household. You can usually turn ACR off in the TV's settings.

Sections 13.5–13.6

Privacy Regulation and Data Governance

Privacy law is a moving target that changes by country and even by state.

EU

GDPR

Europe's sweeping privacy law requires consent and lets people see or delete their data. Fines can reach 4% of global revenue. Meta was fined a record €1.2 billion in 2023.

U.S.

State laws

There's no single federal privacy law. California's CCPA/CPRA led the way, and other states have followed with their own rules.

Side effect

Cookie banner fatigue

GDPR's consent pop-ups are everywhere, and most people click “accept” without reading. Rules don't always work as intended.

Building good data governance

Governance means monitoring and controlling how an organization uses its information systems. Data compliance means following the laws and standards that apply. Strong policies:

  • Involve many groups: IT, legal, security, marketing, HR, and executives.
  • Define what data is collected, why, who can access it, how long it's kept, and how it's protected.
  • Work with regulators and industry groups (like the IAB) proactively instead of waiting for fines.
  • Get audited regularly and are enforced with real consequences.
Vocabulary

Key Terms

Impression
One time an ad is shown to a user.
Display ad
A graphical (image-based) online ad.
Rich media ad
An online ad with animation, audio, or video.
Interstitial
An ad shown before a user reaches the page or screen they wanted.
Creative
The design and content of an advertisement.
CPM
Cost per mille: what an advertiser pays each time an ad is shown 1,000 times.
Cost-per-click (CPC)
A pricing model where advertisers pay only when someone clicks the ad.
Cost-per-action (CPA)
A pricing model where advertisers pay only when a user takes a specific action, like buying.
Affiliate program
A cost-per-action program that pays referring sites a share of the sales they bring in.
Zero-party data
Data a customer intentionally shares with a firm.
First-party data
Data a firm collects from customers' interactions with it.
Second-party data
One firm's customer data shared with a partner.
Third-party data
Data collected by a firm with no direct relationship to the customer.
Cookie
A small piece of identifying text a website stores in your browser.
Third-party cookie
A cookie set by a company other than the site you're visiting, often to track you across sites.
Tracking pixel
An invisible image that reports when a page or email is viewed.
Retargeting
Showing ads on other sites to people who already visited an advertiser's site.
Fingerprinting
Identifying a device from its unique combination of settings and characteristics.
Ad network
A service connecting advertisers with many websites and apps that host ads.
IDFA
Apple's device identifier that apps used to track user activity.
ATT
App Tracking Transparency: Apple's rule that apps must ask permission before tracking users.
Geotargeting
Delivering content or ads based on a user's location.
Geofencing
Using a virtual boundary to trigger an action when a device enters or leaves an area.
VPN
Virtual private network: an encrypted connection that hides your real location and traffic.
ACR
Automated content recognition: technology that identifies what's playing on a screen.
DAI
Dynamic ad insertion: inserting targeted ads into video or TV streams.
GDPR
The EU's General Data Protection Regulation, a strict privacy law with large fines.
Governance
Monitoring and controlling how an organization uses its technology and data.
